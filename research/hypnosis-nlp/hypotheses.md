## H1: The Dual-Route Dissociation Hypothesis

**Claim:** Highly hypnotizable individuals segregate into two neurally distinct subpopulations — *dissociators* (frontal decoupling, alpha suppression) and *absorbers* (enhanced executive engagement, alpha amplification) — that achieve equivalent behavioral hypnotic responses through independent neural mechanisms.

**Basis:** PMC12265433 found that equally hypnotizable HD and LD subjects show opposite alpha power trajectories during hypnosis. PMC13024316's integrative model treats hypnotic responding as unitary. These are incompatible unless there are multiple routes.

**Test Protocol:**
1. Screen N=120 participants with both Harvard Group Scale (hypnotizability) AND DES-II (dissociative experiences)
2. Select 30 high-hypnotizable/high-dissociation, 30 high-hypnotizable/low-dissociation, 30 moderate-hypnotizable, 30 low-hypnotizable
3. Record 64-channel EEG during standardized hypnotic induction + identical suggestion battery
4. Measure: alpha power trajectories, frontal-parietal coherence, theta connectivity, behavioral response scores
5. **Predicted outcome:** The two high-hypnotizable groups will show statistically distinct alpha trajectories (opposite directions) and frontal connectivity patterns while achieving equivalent behavioral scores. Moderate-hypnotizable participants will cluster toward one subtype or the other, not occupy a middle ground.

**Falsification:** If all high-hypnotizable participants show the same alpha trajectory regardless of dissociative capacity, the single-route model holds.

## H2: The Frame-Primacy Hypothesis (Language as Network Switch)

**Claim:** The categorical label "hypnosis" activates the triple network reconfiguration (DMN suppression + ECN-SaN coupling) within 30 seconds of delivery, BEFORE any induction content is processed, and this frame-activation accounts for >50% of the total neural response attributed to hypnotic induction.

**Basis:** PMC12595402 showed white noise labeled "hypnosis" produced depth ratings comparable to real inductions. PMC12606380 showed sham hypnosis achieved 77% of active treatment effect. But no study has measured the TEMPORAL dynamics — does the frame activate the networks before or after the content?

**Test Protocol:**
1. N=80 participants, within-subjects design across 4 sessions (counterbalanced)
2. Conditions: (A) "Hypnosis" label → 30s silence → conventional induction; (B) "Relaxation exercise" label → 30s silence → identical induction; (C) "Hypnosis" label → 30s silence → white noise; (D) "Listening task" label → 30s silence → white noise
3. Continuous fMRI throughout, with DMN/ECN/SaN ROI extraction at 5-second intervals
4. **Critical measurement window:** The 30-second silence after label delivery but before content onset
5. **Predicted outcome:** Conditions A and C will show significant DMN suppression and ECN-SaN coupling increase during the 30s silence period, while B and D will not. The magnitude of network reconfiguration during the silence window will predict >50% of variance in subsequent hypnotic depth ratings.

**Falsification:** If network changes only emerge after induction content begins, or if the label explains <20% of variance, the frame-primacy model fails.

## H3: The Neuroplasticity Window Hypothesis (Trance as Preparation, Not Treatment)

**Claim:** The therapeutic effects of hypnosis occur primarily during the **de-hypnotization / reorientation phase**, not during the hypnotic state itself. The trance state opens a neuroplasticity window (analogous to ketamine's post-dissociative plasticity window) that makes subsequent cognitive reorganization possible.

**Basis:** PMC12497829 demonstrates that ketamine's therapeutic mechanism is the plasticity window AFTER dissociation, not the dissociation itself. PMC13024316 describes hypnosis facilitating "reorganization of self-related cognition" through temporary DMN suppression. No study has tested whether therapeutic effects require the return-to-baseline transition or could be achieved by maintaining trance indefinitely.

**Test Protocol:**
1. N=90 chronic pain patients, randomized to 3 conditions over 6 sessions
2. Condition A: Standard hypnosis (induction → therapeutic suggestion → de-hypnotization → 10min eyes-open integration)
3. Condition B: Extended trance (induction → therapeutic suggestion → maintained trance for 10 additional minutes → abrupt termination, no integration)
4. Condition C: Trance + structured reorientation (induction → therapeutic suggestion → guided de-hypnotization → 10min structured cognitive integration exercise)
5. Measure: Pain scores (BPI) at baseline, post-session, 24h, 1 week, 4 weeks
6. **Predicted outcome:** Condition C > A > B at 4-week follow-up. The structured reorientation phase will be the strongest predictor of durable outcomes. Condition B (abrupt termination) will show acute relief equivalent to A but significantly worse durability.

**Falsification:** If Condition B shows equivalent 4-week outcomes to A and C, the trance state itself is sufficient and no plasticity window is required.

## H4: The Network Architecture Predisposition Hypothesis (Why FND Patients Over-Respond)

**Claim:** Individual response to hypnosis is predicted by baseline DMN-Salience Network connectivity architecture, independent of measured trait suggestibility. Specifically, individuals with higher baseline DMN-SaN coupling (as seen in FND patients) will show larger hypnotic responses because their network starting point is closer to the hypnotic configuration.

**Basis:** PMC12652343 shows FND patients respond to hypnosis at 87% rates despite normal suggestibility scores. PMC12258340 documents aberrant DMN-SaN connectivity in FND. PMC12982806 found no correlation between hypnotizability and treatment outcomes. The connecting thread: baseline network architecture, not trait suggestibility, determines response magnitude.

**Test Protocol:**
1. N=100 healthy participants + N=30 FND patients
2. Baseline: Resting-state fMRI (DMN-SaN-ECN connectivity extraction) + Stanford Hypnotic Susceptibility Scale + DES-II
3. Single standardized hypnosis session with multimodal outcome measurement (subjective depth, behavioral responses, pain modulation, autonomic markers)
4. Regression model: Predict hypnotic response from (a) suggestibility score alone, (b) baseline DMN-SaN connectivity alone, (c) combined model
5. **Predicted outcome:** Model (b) will explain significantly more variance than model (a). FND patients will show the highest hypnotic responses despite normal suggestibility scores, and their elevated response will be statistically mediated by baseline DMN-SaN coupling. Within healthy participants, a subgroup with "FND-like" high DMN-SaN coupling will show similarly elevated responses.

**Falsification:** If suggestibility scores predict response better than network architecture, or if FND patients' elevated response is not mediated by connectivity, the trait model survives.

## H5: The Computational Signature Hypothesis (Hypnotic Language Has Measurable Formal Properties)

**Claim:** Effective hypnotic language has a distinct and quantifiable computational fingerprint — characterized by elevated syntactic embedding depth, increased use of presuppositional constructions, reduced lexical specificity (higher type-token ratio of abstract vs. concrete nouns), and temporal deixis patterns (progressive aspect, future-oriented framing) — that distinguishes it from therapeutic non-hypnotic language and correlates with induction efficacy.

**Basis:** PMC12767259 documents specific linguistic features (permissive framing, metaphor embedding, conditional constructions). PMC13024316 describes Ericksonian indirect suggestion as formally different from direct speech acts. PMC12595402 shows induction content matters beyond labeling. Yet NO paper in the 50-paper corpus has performed computational linguistic analysis of hypnotic scripts. This is the single largest methodological gap.

**Test Protocol:**
1. Corpus assembly: Collect N=200 hypnotic induction transcripts (100 effective, 100 less effective, rated by outcomes data) + N=200 matched therapeutic non-hypnotic transcripts (CBT, motivational interviewing)
2. NLP feature extraction: Parse all transcripts for syntactic depth (dependency parse trees), presupposition density, lexical abstractness (WordNet depth), temporal aspect distribution, hedging frequency, conditional construction rate, metaphor density (using automated metaphor detection), speech rate, pause distribution
3. Classification: Train classifier (Random Forest + BERT fine-tuned) to distinguish effective hypnotic vs. non-hypnotic therapeutic language
4. Validation: Test classifier on held-out hypnotic scripts; correlate predicted "hypnotic score" with actual physiological and behavioral outcomes in a new sample (N=60 participants receiving scripts ranked by classifier)
5. **Predicted outcome:** The classifier will achieve >80% accuracy distinguishing hypnotic from non-hypnotic therapeutic language. At least 3 of the proposed features (syntactic embedding, presupposition density, temporal deixis) will be among the top-10 discriminating features. Scripts with higher "hypnotic scores" will produce deeper physiological hypnotic responses in the validation sample.

**Falsification:** If hypnotic language cannot be distinguished from CBT/MI language at >60% accuracy, or if the identified features do not predict physiological responses, then hypnotic language has no formal computational signature and its effects are entirely context-dependent.
