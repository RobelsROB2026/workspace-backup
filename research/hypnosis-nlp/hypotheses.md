## H1: The Dual-Route Dissociation Hypothesis

**Claim:** Highly hypnotizable individuals segregate into two neurally distinct subpopulations — *dissociators* (frontal decoupling, alpha suppression) and *absorbers* (enhanced executive engagement, alpha amplification) — that achieve equivalent behavioral hypnotic responses through independent neural mechanisms.

**Basis:** PMC12265433 found that equally hypnotizable HD and LD subjects show opposite alpha power trajectories during hypnosis. PMC13024316's integrative model treats hypnotic responding as unitary. These are incompatible unless there are multiple routes.

**Test Protocol:**
1. Screen N=120 participants with both Harvard Group Scale (hypnotizability) AND DES-II (dissociative experiences)
2. Select 30 high-hypnotizable/high-dissociation, 30 high-hypnotizable/low-dissociation, 30 moderate-hypnotizable, 30 low-hypnotizable
3. Record 64-channel EEG during standardized hypnotic induction + identical suggestion battery
4. Measure: alpha power trajectories, frontal-parietal coherence, theta connectivity, behavioral response scores
5. **Predicted outcome:** The two high-hypnotizable groups will show statistically distinct alpha trajectories (opposite directions) and frontal connectivity patterns while achieving equivalent behavioral scores. Moderate-hypnotizable participants will cluster toward one subtype or the other, not occupy a middle ground.

**Falsification:** If all high-hypnotizable participants show the same alpha trajectory regardless of dissociative capacity, the single-route model holds.

## H2: The Frame-Primacy Hypothesis (Language as Network Switch)

**Claim:** The categorical label "hypnosis" activates the triple network reconfiguration (DMN suppression + ECN-SaN coupling) within 30 seconds of delivery, BEFORE any induction content is processed, and this frame-activation accounts for >50% of the total neural response attributed to hypnotic induction.

**Basis:** PMC12595402 showed white noise labeled "hypnosis" produced depth ratings comparable to real inductions. PMC12606380 showed sham hypnosis achieved 77% of active treatment effect. But no study has measured the TEMPORAL dynamics — does the frame activate the networks before or after the content?

**Test Protocol:**
1. N=80 participants, within-subjects design across 4 sessions (counterbalanced)
2. Conditions: (A) "Hypnosis" label → 30s silence → conventional induction; (B) "Relaxation exercise" label → 30s silence → identical induction; (C) "Hypnosis" label → 30s silence → white noise; (D) "Listening task" label → 30s silence → white noise
3. Continuous fMRI throughout, with DMN/ECN/SaN ROI extraction at 5-second intervals
4. **Critical measurement window:** The 30-second silence after label delivery but before content onset
5. **Predicted outcome:** Conditions A and C will show significant DMN suppression and ECN-SaN coupling increase during the 30s silence period, while B and D will not. The magnitude of network reconfiguration during the silence window will predict >50% of variance in subsequent hypnotic depth ratings.

**Falsification:** If network changes only emerge after induction content begins, or if the label explains <20% of variance, the frame-primacy model fails.

## H3: The Neuroplasticity Window Hypothesis (Trance as Preparation, Not Treatment)

**Claim:** The therapeutic effects of hypnosis occur primarily during the **de-hypnotization / reorientation phase**, not during the hypnotic state itself. The trance state opens a neuroplasticity window (analogous to ketamine's post-dissociative plasticity window) that makes subsequent cognitive reorganization possible.

**Basis:** PMC12497829 demonstrates that ketamine's therapeutic mechanism is the plasticity window AFTER dissociation, not the dissociation itself. PMC13024316 describes hypnosis facilitating "reorganization of self-related cognition" through temporary DMN suppression. No study has tested whether therapeutic effects require the return-to-baseline transition or could be achieved by maintaining trance indefinitely.

**Test Protocol:**
1. N=90 chronic pain patients, randomized to 3 conditions over 6 sessions
2. Condition A: Standard hypnosis (induction → therapeutic suggestion → de-hypnotization → 10min eyes-open integration)
3. Condition B: Extended trance (induction → therapeutic suggestion → maintained trance for 10 additional minutes → abrupt termination, no integration)
4. Condition C: Trance + structured reorientation (induction → therapeutic suggestion → guided de-hypnotization → 10min structured cognitive integration exercise)
5. Measure: Pain scores (BPI) at baseline, post-session, 24h, 1 week, 4 weeks
6. **Predicted outcome:** Condition C > A > B at 4-week follow-up. The structured reorientation phase will be the strongest predictor of durable outcomes. Condition B (abrupt termination) will show acute relief equivalent to A but significantly worse durability.

**Falsification:** If Condition B shows equivalent 4-week outcomes to A and C, the trance state itself is sufficient and no plasticity window is required.

## H4: The Network Architecture Predisposition Hypothesis (Why FND Patients Over-Respond)

**Claim:** Individual response to hypnosis is predicted by baseline DMN-Salience Network connectivity architecture, independent of measured trait suggestibility. Specifically, individuals with higher baseline DMN-SaN coupling (as seen in FND patients) will show larger hypnotic responses because their network starting point is closer to the hypnotic configuration.

**Basis:** PMC12652343 shows FND patients respond to hypnosis at 87% rates despite normal suggestibility scores. PMC12258340 documents aberrant DMN-SaN connectivity in FND. PMC12982806 found no correlation between hypnotizability and treatment outcomes. The connecting thread: baseline network architecture, not trait suggestibility, determines response magnitude.

**Test Protocol:**
1. N=100 healthy participants + N=30 FND patients
2. Baseline: Resting-state fMRI (DMN-SaN-ECN connectivity extraction) + Stanford Hypnotic Susceptibility Scale + DES-II
3. Single standardized hypnosis session with multimodal outcome measurement (subjective depth, behavioral responses, pain modulation, autonomic markers)
4. Regression model: Predict hypnotic response from (a) suggestibility score alone, (b) baseline DMN-SaN connectivity alone, (c) combined model
5. **Predicted outcome:** Model (b) will explain significantly more variance than model (a). FND patients will show the highest hypnotic responses despite normal suggestibility scores, and their elevated response will be statistically mediated by baseline DMN-SaN coupling. Within healthy participants, a subgroup with "FND-like" high DMN-SaN coupling will show similarly elevated responses.

**Falsification:** If suggestibility scores predict response better than network architecture, or if FND patients' elevated response is not mediated by connectivity, the trait model survives.

## H5: The Computational Signature Hypothesis (Hypnotic Language Has Measurable Formal Properties)

**Claim:** Effective hypnotic language has a distinct and quantifiable computational fingerprint — characterized by elevated syntactic embedding depth, increased use of presuppositional constructions, reduced lexical specificity (higher type-token ratio of abstract vs. concrete nouns), and temporal deixis patterns (progressive aspect, future-oriented framing) — that distinguishes it from therapeutic non-hypnotic language and correlates with induction efficacy.

**Basis:** PMC12767259 documents specific linguistic features (permissive framing, metaphor embedding, conditional constructions). PMC13024316 describes Ericksonian indirect suggestion as formally different from direct speech acts. PMC12595402 shows induction content matters beyond labeling. Yet NO paper in the 50-paper corpus has performed computational linguistic analysis of hypnotic scripts. This is the single largest methodological gap.

**Test Protocol:**
1. Corpus assembly: Collect N=200 hypnotic induction transcripts (100 effective, 100 less effective, rated by outcomes data) + N=200 matched therapeutic non-hypnotic transcripts (CBT, motivational interviewing)
2. NLP feature extraction: Parse all transcripts for syntactic depth (dependency parse trees), presupposition density, lexical abstractness (WordNet depth), temporal aspect distribution, hedging frequency, conditional construction rate, metaphor density (using automated metaphor detection), speech rate, pause distribution
3. Classification: Train classifier (Random Forest + BERT fine-tuned) to distinguish effective hypnotic vs. non-hypnotic therapeutic language
4. Validation: Test classifier on held-out hypnotic scripts; correlate predicted "hypnotic score" with actual physiological and behavioral outcomes in a new sample (N=60 participants receiving scripts ranked by classifier)
5. **Predicted outcome:** The classifier will achieve >80% accuracy distinguishing hypnotic from non-hypnotic therapeutic language. At least 3 of the proposed features (syntactic embedding, presupposition density, temporal deixis) will be among the top-10 discriminating features. Scripts with higher "hypnotic scores" will produce deeper physiological hypnotic responses in the validation sample.

**Falsification:** If hypnotic language cannot be distinguished from CBT/MI language at >60% accuracy, or if the identified features do not predict physiological responses, then hypnotic language has no formal computational signature and its effects are entirely context-dependent.

---

*These hypotheses are ordered by proximity to testability: H1 and H2 could be executed with existing lab infrastructure. H3 requires a clinical trial. H4 requires an FND patient cohort. H5 requires corpus assembly + NLP pipeline development but would open an entirely new methodological front.*

## H6: The Pupil Spectral Signature Hypothesis (Frequency, Not Amplitude)

**Claim:** Hypnotic depth — and the dual-route architecture (H1) — are encoded in the FREQUENCY DOMAIN and NON-LINEAR DYNAMICS of the pupil signal, not in pupil diameter. Specifically: (a) Pupil Band Median Frequency (BMF) follows a U-shaped function of hypnotizability, with mediums differing from both highs and lows in the same direction. (b) Recurrence Plot Entropy of the pupil signal increases during induction only in highs. (c) These spectral/non-linear features predict susceptibility-class membership better than the Stanford SHSS or HIP at the individual level.

**Basis:** PMC7970859 found NO significant difference in resting pupil diameter between high, medium, and low hypnotizables (N = 41) — falsifying the simple "highs have higher tonic LC tone" hypothesis. But the same study found a U-shaped relationship in pupil BMF (mediums > highs and lows at session start) and an increase in pupil signal entropy across the session that was significant only in highs (F(2,38) = 3.907, p = 0.029). The authors treated this as a secondary finding because it did not survive Bonferroni correction (p > 0.025) — yet the pattern is directionally consistent and biologically plausible. No subsequent study has replicated or extended this frequency-domain approach.

**Test Protocol:**
1. N = 120 healthy participants, screened with Stanford Hypnotic Susceptibility Scale (SHSS-A) into highs (≥8), mediums (5-7), lows (<4), n ≈ 40 per group
2. Three sessions per participant (counterbalanced): (A) eyes-open resting baseline, (B) standard hypnotic induction (Elman-style), (C) sham induction (white noise labeled "hypnosis" per PMC12595402)
3. Continuous binocular pupillometry at ≥100 Hz throughout all sessions in a luminance-controlled chamber. Concurrent ECG, EDA, respiration
4. Feature extraction per 60-second window: median pupil diameter, MAD, BMF, Recurrence Plot Determinism, Recurrence Plot Entropy, ApEn, sample entropy
5. **Predicted outcomes:**
   - Pupil diameter alone will not discriminate hypnotizability groups (replicating PMC7970859 null)
   - Pupil BMF will show a U-shaped function of hypnotizability with statistical significance at session start
   - RP Entropy will increase across the induction session significantly more in highs than in mediums or lows
   - A classifier using ONLY pupil frequency-domain features will predict SHSS class with ≥70% accuracy
   - Sham induction (condition C) will produce smaller pupil-spectral shifts than real induction even though subjective depth ratings are equivalent (closing the PMC12606380 / PMC12595402 paradox: the frame produces clinical effect but NOT spectral signature)

**Falsification:** If pupil diameter discriminates groups better than spectral features, or if the U-shaped BMF pattern fails to replicate, the frequency-signature hypothesis fails. If the sham induction produces equivalent pupil-spectral shifts to real induction, the frame-vs-content distinction (Open Q1) collapses and the pupil signal is not a useful objective marker.

**Why this matters:** This is the first proposal to use pupil spectral/non-linear features as a hypnosis biomarker. If it works, it provides an objective, continuous, non-invasive depth signal that could drive H8's biometric closed-loop delivery system. It also disambiguates the EEG-behavior gap (Open Q1) by potentially being the signal that DOES distinguish framed-only from full inductions.

## H7: The Pupil-Cardiac Dissociation Hypothesis (Two Autonomic Channels, Independently Controlled)

**Claim:** Hypnotic depth is not a scalar. It is a 2D state defined by INDEPENDENT control of (a) the LC-noradrenergic axis (indexed by pupil spectral content) and (b) the vagal-cardiovascular axis (indexed by RMSSD, HF-HRV). Skilled hypnotists modulate these two channels in ORTHOGONAL DIRECTIONS, and this orthogonal modulation — not the average autonomic shift — is the true marker of effective induction. The cybernetic layer's wait-for-exhalation markers (mesh Section 8.1) lock onto the cardiovascular axis; the trigeminal-recruitment patterns (eye fixation, jaw release) lock onto the LC axis. A skilled live hypnotist plays both channels independently; a static script can only play one.

**Basis:** PMC7970859 documented that pupil indicators shifted toward sympathetic while cardiac indicators shifted toward parasympathetic during the same relaxation session, and that highs showed DECREASED RMSSD (opposite to mediums) — meaning relaxation as a cognitive task costs vagal tone in highs. This is incompatible with the unified "challenge autonomic profile" framing in PMC12982806 / mesh Section 4. The existing "depth of trance" measurements average these two axes together, hiding the structure.

**Test Protocol:**
1. Recruit 30 expert hypnotists (≥10 years clinical practice) and 30 novice hypnotists (<2 years), N = 60 hypnotists total
2. Each hypnotist runs 4 induction sessions with naive subjects (N = 240 sessions total). Subjects pre-screened with SHSS-A
3. Continuous dual measurement during all sessions: pupillometry (≥100 Hz) + ECG (RMSSD, HF-HRV in 60s windows)
4. Linguistic annotation of each session for: trigeminal-recruitment markers (eye/face/jaw references), wait-for-exhalation markers, embedded commands per 60s window
5. **Predicted outcomes:**
   - Expert hypnotists produce 2D pupil-cardiac trajectories that span both axes orthogonally; novices produce trajectories that move primarily along one axis only
   - The orthogonality score (1 - |correlation(pupil-axis, cardiac-axis)|) predicts subjective depth ratings AND behavioral suggestion-response scores better than either single-axis measurement
   - Trigeminal-recruitment marker density in the linguistic transcript correlates with pupil-axis shift but NOT cardiac-axis shift
   - Wait-for-exhalation marker density correlates with cardiac-axis shift but NOT pupil-axis shift
   - Static scripts (read aloud without feedback) produce 1D trajectories regardless of script content
6. Cross-validation: predict expert vs. novice from pupil-cardiac orthogonality with ≥80% accuracy

**Falsification:** If pupil and cardiac signals are correlated during hypnosis (single axis), or if expert and novice hypnotists produce indistinguishable 2D trajectories, the dual-channel model fails. If trigeminal-recruitment markers in language are uncorrelated with pupil dynamics, the linguistic-physiological mapping fails.

**Why this matters:** This redefines the cybernetic model (mesh Section III). The 37x cybernetic-marker density measured in live sessions [phase2_cybernetic_analysis.md] decomposes into TWO control loops, not one — pupil-axis (LC, attended cues, trigeminal entry) and cardiac-axis (vagal, respiratory phase). Effective hypnosis requires controlling both. Digital delivery (Open Q8) becomes a two-controller engineering problem.

## H8: The Pupillometric Closed-Loop Delivery Hypothesis (Recovering the Cybernetic Layer Digitally)

**Claim:** A digital hypnosis system that uses real-time pupillometry to gate suggestion delivery — specifically, advancing the script only when pupil Recurrence Plot Entropy exceeds a participant-calibrated threshold — will produce greater hypnotic depth (subjective and behavioral), greater autonomic shift (RMSSD, EDA), and greater hypoalgesia (cold pressor pain ratings) than the same script delivered at fixed tempo. This is the minimum-viable cybernetic recovery for app-delivered hypnosis (mesh Open Q8).

**Basis:** Mesh Section III documents 74.2 cybernetic markers per 1,000 words in live sessions vs. ~2/1k in scripts, with wait-for-exhalation markers being the largest single component (44.8/1k). The pupil-cardiac dissociation hypothesis (H7) predicts that pupil dynamics index the cognitive/attentional channel that breathing-based pacing does not capture. PMC7970859 showed pupil RP Entropy increases during induction in highs — a candidate continuous depth signal. PMC10067681 documented that PDR is the clinical gold-standard for nociception monitoring during anesthesia using off-the-shelf infrared pupillometers — the technology is mature and commercially available.

**Test Protocol:**
1. N = 80 participants, randomized to 4 conditions (between-subjects, n = 20 each)
2. All conditions: identical 12-minute Elman-style induction with hypnoanalgesia suggestion, followed by cold pressor pain task (hand in 4°C water for 90s)
3. Conditions:
   - (A) Fixed-tempo audio (standard digital app baseline)
   - (B) Breathing-gated audio (suggestions advance only on exhalation, captured via chest strap)
   - (C) Pupil-gated audio (suggestions advance only when 30s rolling pupil RP Entropy exceeds participant's resting +1 SD)
   - (D) Dual-gated audio (advance requires BOTH breathing AND pupil conditions — minimum-viable two-controller cybernetic layer per H7)
4. Outcomes: Stanford Hypnotic Clinical Scale depth rating, cold pressor pain VAS (0-100), RMSSD change, EDA tonic change, post-session subjective rapport rating
5. **Predicted outcomes:**
   - Condition D > C > B > A on all outcomes
   - The depth-rating gain from C over A will be partially explained by pupil-cardiac orthogonality (per H7)
   - Condition D will approach in-person hypnosis effect sizes from PMC12862456 / PMC12611927 within ±20%
   - Condition A (existing app baseline) will produce only ~50% of D's pain reduction effect

**Falsification:** If conditions B, C, and D are statistically indistinguishable from A, the cybernetic layer cannot be recovered through biometric gating and live presence is doing something else (perhaps social/relational rather than cybernetic). If C ≤ B, then pupil dynamics add nothing beyond breathing — the H7 dual-channel model would be weakened.

**Why this matters:** This is the engineering proof-of-concept for biometric-responsive digital hypnosis. If it works, it transforms an 84,395-user-scale app delivery from a Phase 1 open-loop system (mesh Section 10.1) into a Phase 2 closed-loop system (Section 10.2) without requiring a live hypnotist. It also directly tests the cybernetic-density-as-efficacy-predictor hypothesis (Open Q9) by manipulating cybernetic density experimentally.
