TYPE: Ericksonian conversational induction — age regression metaphor
PARTICIPANTS: Hypnotist, Subject
FORMAT: Extended monologue with minimal subject responses and non-verbal feedback notes

HYPNOTIST: Okay, well, why don't you just look at the far upper corner of that frame there… The far upper corner of that frame.

[Subject fixates on the corner]

HYPNOTIST: …do you see how the light is dancing around the corner of the frame?

SUBJECT: Yeah.

HYPNOTIST: That's right. And you can just keep your eyes there and notice as you look at that corner how interesting it is what happens around the edge when you really focus.

Now, I wonder if you can remember… your first day, or your first week, of going to school. You know, when you went to school there were so many things to learn. Do you remember your teacher? I don't know whether you remember your first teacher or not. But there was your teacher. And there were the other children sitting around you in that classroom. And you were looking around, trying to understand.

And you learned to recognize letters. And at first those letters were just shapes. Strange marks on the page. And some of them looked so similar. How do you tell an M from an N? Or a Q from an O? But somehow, over time, they became different. They became letters. They had sounds. And those sounds went together to make words. And those words went together to make sentences.

And it can seem like it is just too much to ask of you. When you think about everything you had to learn. And yet you did. And I wonder if you know how remarkable that is. That your mind was able to take all of those shapes and sounds and put them together and make meaning from them.

And then there was learning to write. That was even harder, wasn't it? Because now you had to form those shapes. Your hand had to learn to move in the right way. And you had to hold the pencil just so. And at first the letters were big and wobbly and they didn't look like letters at all. But you practiced and practiced and gradually they became smaller and neater and eventually they looked like letters. And then words. And then sentences.

And as you think about that now, perhaps you can notice something happening with your eyes. [Subject's eyelids begin to twitch] That's right. Because as you access those early memories, those early learning experiences, something happens naturally. The eyes get tired. The lids get heavy. Because you're going inside.

And you can let those eyes close whenever they want to. There's no need to fight it. Just let it happen naturally. As naturally as learning happened. As naturally as breathing happens.

[Subject's eyes close]

HYPNOTIST: That's right. And you can continue to go inside. And your unconscious mind can relearn those lessons about learning. About how much you can learn without even trying. About how the mind can absorb and integrate and understand in its own way, in its own time.

And there is a part of you that knows about curiosity. And creativity. And the joy of discovery. And those experiences are there for you now, flowing, growing, enjoying those experiences. And you can let yourself drift deeper, knowing that the mind learns best when it's relaxed, when it's open, when it feels safe. Just as it did then.
